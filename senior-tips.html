Tips, stories and info to know

As Mary Kennedy wheeled a small cart of groceries up to her apartment building, a cat wound its way through her legs. The skinny black creature knows who feeds it.

Felines aren’t the only ones benefiting from Kennedy’s recent trip to the food bank, where she got tuna and cat food. She also picked up packs of raisins to pass out to the neighborhood kids, fruit cups for her blind neighbor who is nervous to leave the building and hot dog buns for an upcoming barbecue.

Mary’s tips

  • The Animal Protective League offers free cat and dog food every Tuesday, 10 am - 1 pm at the food bank’s location on Waterloo.
  • Don’t take RTA’s para-transit with a buggy like the one above for groceries. They don’t allow it.
  • Senior Transportation Connection can transport you to the grocery story or food bank. Just call 3 days ahead.
  • Wal-Mart has some affordable gluten-free options.

Is SNAP shrinking?

Seniors across Cleveland are frustrated with puny SNAP benefits. Anna Washington said she only gets $26 a month — enough for a “loaf of bread and milk,” she joked. And many say they’ve noticed their allotment shrinking in recent years, despite grocery prices growing. Valeria Ray used to get $100 but now gets $24.

Experts suspect residents are seeing their benefits shrink because COVID benefits expired in 2023. Older adults also may get lower SNAP benefits if they have income from a disability check or social security.

Short answer: Sort of.

Anna’s tips

  • If your SNAP shrinks, check whether you get any benefits through your health insurance plan. Anna gets $287 through CareSource.
  • Try an electric can opener if it’s difficult to open one on your own.

On most nights, Mario Esquivel heats up leftover “crockpot special” in his microwave — chicken he stewed alongside whatever veggies, beans and rice he has on hand. He even added papaya once, after his building got a free delivery of it.

Esquivel spent years working as a chef but was laid off after COVID. After that, he got back surgery. He can still walk, but he typically uses a rollator to get around. The crockpot is his solution.

“If I put it in a crock pot, I’m not over the stove all day,” Esquivel said.

Mario’s tips

  • Crockpot meals can be delicious and simpler to make, if you want to avoid standing for long periods of time.
  • Volunteering at local food pantries may mean you get sent home with food of your own.
  • Check out your health insurance plan. Some allow you to accumulate points for going to doctor’s appointments, which can be redeemed for gift cards.
